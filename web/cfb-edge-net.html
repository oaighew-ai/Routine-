CFB Edge · fee-adjusted board

Your gross edge is not your edge. The fee is.

Kalshi charges round_up(0.07 × C × P × (1−P)), which peaks at 1.75¢ per contract at a coin flip. A cross-market gate finds most of its candidates right there, at 47–49¢, because that is where a book posting −110 both ways de-vigs to exactly 50. Those are the trades the fee eats. Method

Fee

fee¢ = 7 × P × (1−P) where P is the price in dollars. Kalshi rounds the order total up to the next cent; on any real size that rounding washes out, so the per-contract figure is the one to reason with. Checked against Kalshi's own published example: 20 contracts at 60¢ costs $0.34.

The entry bar has to bend

Clearing 1¢ of net edge needs 2.75¢ gross at a coin flip but only 2.16¢ at 21¢ and 1.63¢ at 10¢. A flat cent bar is loosest exactly where your gate finds the most candidates. Use required_gross = target_net + 7×P×(1−P).

Stake

For a contract bought at P cents that settles at 100, full Kelly reduces to f* = net¢ ÷ (100 − P¢). Shown here at a quarter of that and capped at 2% of bankroll, because the edge is estimated, not known.

What this does not tell you

Fees explain why the leans are smaller than posted. They do not establish that the remainder is real. Rows marked assumed 50¢ rest on the book pricing that number −110 both ways; a shaded line breaks them outright. The 0.07 coefficient is the documented general case and some products differ, so read the rate off the account before staking. Nothing here is validated by closing line value.
