Four plays, on lines nobody recorded. A shortlist, not a card.

The signal takes exactly one market input: the number a book posts when it first opens a game. Seven of those were found by search on Sept 14 and four clear the bar. None came from the capture, so for six of the seven there is no way to tell an opening number from one that has already moved.

This matters more than it sounds. The model cannot beat a closing line at all, measured at an incremental coefficient of −0.02 against 6,398 games. What survives is predicting where a line travels: 0.44 points of closing line value measured from the open. A card built after the market has moved is a card built on value someone else already collected, and there is no way to recover it.

Week 2 is the demonstration. Oklahoma at Michigan opened Michigan −2.5, the model said Michigan was overvalued, and the line travelled nine points to Oklahoma −6.5. The model was right and the bet was never available, because nobody was recording when the number posted.

North Carolina at Clemson is this week's version, and the most useful row on the page. One source named the open, Clemson −5.5, and the current number, −8.5, separately. At the open the gap was 0.55; at the current number it is 3.55. Neither clears the bar, a three-point move happened, and the model had nothing to say at either end. That is what most of a real week looks like.

The release clock

DoneSun 18:00 UTC

Confirm the capture is recording, not just running

A task that starts, fails to authenticate and exits looks identical to a quiet board: both leave you with nothing. The task status does not answer this. The count of recorded markets does.

  1. python -m cfb_edge.watch --log data\opens.jsonl.gz --rebuild --out data\opens.csv prints how many markets have a recorded open. Non-zero means the chain works end to end. Zero, an hour into the window, means it does not, whatever Get-ScheduledTask reports.
  2. If it is zero, the usual cause is ODDS_API_KEY set in one terminal rather than as a user or machine variable. A scheduled task does not inherit a terminal's environment.
  3. Watch the credit burn. Billing is one credit per region per market, so the default three regions cost 3 a poll and the schedule makes 652 polls a week: about 1,956 credits weekly. --regions us costs a third and gives up the low-hold European books.

The capture writes to data/opens.jsonl.gz, which is gitignored along with every bet and signal log. That is deliberate: this repository is public and the operator's own record does not belong in it. The consequence is that nothing outside the machine running the capture can see whether it worked.

OpenSun 22:00 UTC

Poll every five minutes through the window

The window is measured, not guessed: Sunday 22:00 UTC onward, all Monday, Tuesday until 18:00. The log records first-seen prices and cannot overwrite them, which is the whole point of running it live rather than reading a board later.

A second Routine fires at 22:00 to verify the capture actually landed. Zero recorded markets an hour into the window means it is not working, whatever the task status says.

Grade against the close, gated on kickoff

Closing lines are taken from the last quote seen before kickoff, not the last quote seen. Without that gate a single in-play quote becomes the close: on a Kansas signal opening at +6.5 with a real close of +5.5, one in-play quote at −17 turned +1.0 points of value into +23.5, biased positive because lines move toward whoever is winning.

Twenty-two real bets, weeks 3 through 13. The noise is 21 times the edge. You will finish the year up or down a few hundred dollars and it will tell you nothing about whether the model works.

That ratio does not improve with a bigger bankroll. Double the money and both numbers double. There is no size at which realised profit becomes the scorecard, which is why it is not the scorecard here.

251 times fewer observations. That is the whole argument for the zero-stake log, and it is why skipping signals on a quiet week costs more than skipping a bet. Bet the card if you want to prove you can get the fill. The answer arrives in the CLV number six weeks from now, not in the balance.

The scorecard, and when it fires

Closing line value is the measure, not win-loss record. Profit at twenty bets is noise. This is a sequential probability ratio test on cumulative CLV: null 0, alternative +0.44, per-bet sd 2.78, α 0.05, β 0.20. Cross the left boundary and the strategy is dead; cross the right and it is confirmed.

kill at −27.37 cumulative points 0 sits 36% along confirm at +48.70

Both boundaries drift with the bet count at a slope of 0.22 points per observation, which is why volume decides how fast this resolves. On real bets alone it is a three-season question. On paper signals at roughly 25 a week it is about a four-week one, and that is the entire reason the zero-stake log exists.
