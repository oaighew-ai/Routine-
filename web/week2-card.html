Added Sept 9: the week gate

The strategy has always been measured in aggregate. It had never been measured by week. Splitting the same 1,375 replicated bets on the week of the season gives two different strategies wearing one name.

Weeks 1 and 2 are not a weaker version of the edge. They are indistinguishable from zero, and the whole of the claimed +0.44 lives from week 3 on. The mechanism is not mysterious: in week 2 most teams have played once and roughly a third have played nothing at all, so their rating is a regressed prior and the model is disagreeing with the market about a number it has no information on. That is exactly the condition that manufactured the Arizona State false signal.

MIN_SEASON_WEEK = 3 in cfb_edge/strategy.py. Weeks 1 and 2 now return no side at all.

A cap on the size of the disagreement was proposed at the same time and the data refused it. The argument was that a 26-point gap means a broken rating, not an edge. Bucketing says the opposite: CLV rises with the gap — +0.099 at 4–6 points, +0.527 at 8–10, +0.572 at 14–20. There is now a test asserting a 26-point gap still fires, so that idea cannot come back by accident.

Corrected Sept 9: these prices were wrong

The first version of this card quoted Kansas at 39¢ and Oklahoma at 67¢. Those were the model's own fair values, not prices anyone was offering. The market was near 26¢ and 55¢.

The pricing code built its margin distribution from the model's projection and read both the contract price and the key-number density off it. That is the one thing the model is measured as having no right to do: against 6,398 real closing lines its incremental coefficient is −0.02 at a t of −0.31.

The net edge on each is about one cent. Filling at the quoted price meant paying twelve cents over the market to collect one, which is roughly ten times the edge in the wrong direction.

At the time this correction was written the plays survived it, because the edge was always the 0.44 points of predicted movement rather than the model's disagreement with the line. The week gate above then removed them for a different reason entirely. Both findings stand: the pricing was wrong, and the week was wrong.

Withdrawn: Arizona State +14.5

This was on the card until today. It was a false signal.

The power ratings came out of a shrinkage fit that compressed them by a factor of 1.40 against real closing lines — projections had a standard deviation of 8.95 where the market's was 13.34. A compressed model pulls every projection toward zero, which makes every underdog look cheap. Setting RATING_SCALE = 1.40 moved the slope from 1.400 to 1.001.

Arizona State's gap fell from 4.64 to 1.98 and it dropped off the card. Oklahoma's rose from 3.49 to 5.17 and it appeared. The correction cost one bad bet and found one good one, and the good one was the largest line move on the board.

Week 3 is the first week that counts

Not a figure of speech. Week 3 is where the measured edge starts, and it is played Sept 19. Its opening lines post from Sunday Sept 13, 22:00 UTC. Two Routines are armed for that Sunday: one at 18:00 UTC to start the capture, one at 22:00 UTC to verify it actually landed.

  1. Put your Odds API key in ODDS_API_KEY as a user or machine variable. A scheduled task does not inherit one set in a single terminal, and this is the most common way the capture silently records nothing.
  2. Check your plan's allowance. At the default three regions the schedule costs about 1,956 credits a week. --regions us costs a third of that and gives up the low-hold European books.
  3. Run scripts\capture.bat, or register the weekly task with scripts\install_capture_task.ps1. The PowerShell has never been run anywhere — there is no Windows in CI — so watch the first registration rather than assuming it took.
  4. Monday, build the card and log the paper signals: cfb_edge signals --week 3. That records every signal on the board at zero stake, about 25 observations a week against the 2 that would get money. The stop rule needs that volume: on real bets alone it would kill a worthless strategy only 8.7% of the time in a season.

Nothing else is worth doing this week. The model is now correct, it caught the nine-point Oklahoma move, and it caught it after the fact because nobody was capturing when the number posted. That is the only fixable part.
