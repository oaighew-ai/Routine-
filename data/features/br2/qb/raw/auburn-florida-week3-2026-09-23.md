###### SERIES HISTORY

• Auburn leads the all-time series against Florida, 
43-39-2, including a 26-8-1 advantage in games 
played on the Plains.

• It will mark the first meeting between the two teams 
at Jordan-Hare Stadium since 2011 (AU, 17-6).

• The two programs played every year from 1945-

2002 but have played only four times since then.

• The Tigers have won four of the last six matchups 
in the series, including three straight at home.

• Five of Auburn’s last seven wins in the series have 
been by three points.

###### RANKED RIVALRY

• Since 1952, this is just the 10th time Auburn and 
Florida have met when both teams are unranked in 
the AP poll in 57 total meetings.

• At least one team has been ranked in 36 of the 
last 41 matchups (since 1967).

• In three of Auburn’s last four wins in the series, the 
Tigers were the lower ranked or unranked team.

• In the 36 meetings when at least one team has 
been ranked, the lower ranked or unranked team 
has won 16 times.

###### FLORIDA CONNECTIONS

• Auburn coaches DJ Durkin, Coleman Hutzler 
and Larry Scott have all previously spent time on 
Florida’s staff in their careers.

- Durkin: Linebackers/Special Teams (2010-12), 
Defensive Coordinator (2013-14)

- Hutzler: Asst Special Teams/Linebackers 
(2010-11), Special Teams Coordinator/Outside 
Linebackers (2014)

- Scott: Tight Ends (2018-19)

• Former Auburn WR and assistant coach Marcus 
Davis is in his first season as the Gators’ outside 
wide receivers coach.

###### REMATCH FROM 2025

• Saturday’s game in a sense is a rematch of the 
2025 matchup between USF and No. 13 Florida, 
resulting in an 18-16 USF win.

• QB Byrum Brown accounted for 329 total yards 
and a touchdown, while a trio of Auburn receivers 
in Christian Neptune, Chas Nimrod and Keshaun 
Singleton combined for 10 receptions, 138 yards 
and a score.

- Brown: 23-for-36, 263 yards, 1 TD;

17 rush, 66 yards

- Neptune: 5 rec, 39 yards

- Nimrod: 3 rec, 24 yards

- Singleton: 2 rec, 75 yards, 1 TD

###### ALL-AMERICAN LINEBACKER XAVIER ATKINS

• Junior Xavier Atkins is first returning All-American 
on defense at Auburn since Tracy Rocker, who 
was a two-time All-American in 1987-88. Atkins has 
received multiple Preseason All-America honors for 
2026 following his stellar 2025 campaign.

• Atkins and Byrum Brown gave Auburn two players 
on the Maxwell Award Preseason List, one of five 
programs in the SEC with multiple selections.
• Atkins (No. 16) and Brown (No. 80) were both on 
On3’s ‘Way-too-Early” Top 100 list for the 2026 season. 
Atkins was the highest rated linebacker on the list.
• Atkins finished the 2025 season with 9.0 sacks and 
17.0 TFL - the most tackles for loss by an Auburn 
player since 2010.

###### PICKING UP WHERE HE LEFT OFF

• Atkins is ninth among active FBS players with 0.90 
TFL per game in his career.

• He is four tackles shy of 100 for his career and 1.0 
TFL shy of 20.0 for his career.

###### THE LAST TIME VS. FLORIDA FLORIDA 24, AUBURN 13

No. 7 Auburn suffered its first loss of the 2019 as the Tigers dropped a 24-13 contest on the road at No. 10 Florida.
The Gators struck first with a long touchdown on their first
possession, but the Auburn defense responded with three
turnovers on the next four Florida drives. Two of the turnovers
led to field goals (48, 39) by Anders Carlson, who made it 7-6
at the end of the first quarter.

Christian Tutt forced the first turnover when he sacked
Florida quarterback Kyle Trask and jarred the ball loose.
Chandler Wooten fell it on it for the for the fumble recovery.
It was the first career forced fumble for Tutt and first career
fumble recovery for Wooten.

Then, on the very next Florida possession, Auburn linebacker K.J. Britt made a big hit on the running back to pop
the ball loose. This time, it was defensive lineman Tyrone
Truesdell who came up with it for his second career fumble
recovery.

And then with Florida knocking on the door late in the
quarter, it was the Auburn defense that came up with yet
another fumble and recovery. Senior Marlon Davidson shot
off the edge and hit Trask just as he was trying to throw the
football. Fellow defensive lineman Derrick Brown picked up
the loose ball and returned it 42 yards.

In records dating back through 1996, this was the first
game the Tigers recovered three fumbles in a quarter.

The Auburn offense found the end zone early in the second quarter when, on the first play after a failed fake punt attempt, quarterback Bo Nix connected with Seth Williams on a
32-yard touchdown pass. It was Nix’s eighth touchdown pass
this season, and the fifth receiving touchdown for Williams,
who now has 10 for his career.

Williams led the Tigers with four catches for 79 yards and
the touchdown.

Auburn still trailed 17-13 at the half, and it stayed that
way for most of the second half until late in the fourth quarter
when Florida extended the lead with an 88-yard touchdown
run from Lamical Perine.

Prior to that, Brown made another game-changing play
early in the fourth quarter when he got to Trask, knocked the
ball away and then grabbed it out of the air for his second
fumble recovery of the game. The last Auburn player to with
two fumble recoveries in a game was Montravius Adams at
Mississippi State in 2016.

As a unit, the Auburn defense finished with seven tackles
for loss, four sacks and the four fumble recoveries. Sophomore linebacker Zakoby McClain led the way with 10 tackles.

**JORDAN-HARE STADIUM // 88,043 23 CONSECUTIVE SELLOUTS**

Pat Dye Field at Jordan-Hare Stadium, the
nation’s 11th-largest on-campus stadium with
a capacity of 88,043, is in its 83rd year as
home to the Auburn Tigers.

On football Saturdays in Auburn, Jordan-
Hare Stadium becomes Alabama’s sixth-largest city. A record number of 63,500 season
tickets were sold before the 2023 and 2024
seasons.

The stadium is named for Ralph “Shug”
Jordan, Auburn’s all-time winningest football
coach, and Clifford Leroy Hare, a member of
Auburn’s first football team, president of the
old Southern Conference and longtime chairman of Auburn’s Faculty Athletic Committee.

Legendary coach Pat Dye has also had
his name forever attached to Jordan-Hare
Stadium, as the turf officially became known
as Pat Dye Field at Jordan-Hare Stadium
prior to the 2005 Iron Bowl. Dye, who served
as coach and athletics director at Auburn
(1981-92) and was inducted into the College
Football Hall of Fame (2005), led Auburn to a
99-39-4 record and four SEC titles in his 12
seasons on the Plains.

**Auburn’s current string of 23 consecutive sellout crowds at Jordan-Hare** 
**Stadium, including the Mercer game, 
breaks the previous program record 
streak of 17 consecutive sellout crowds, 
set between the 2013 Georgia game and 
the 2016 Clemson game.**

What is now Jordan-Hare Stadium was
dedicated November 30, 1939, at the Auburn-
Florida game. Babe McGehee, who passed
away in 2005, scored the first touchdown
in what was then called “Auburn Stadium,”
by catching a pass from Dick McGowen.
McGowen, who later coached at Auburn
under Jordan, kicked the extra point and
Auburn tied Florida, 7-7.

That first stadium held 7,500 seats and
consisted of what is now the bottom part of
the lower west stands. When the stadium was
renamed “Cliff Hare Stadium” in 1949, 14,000
seats—the present lower east stands—had
been added, raising capacity to 21,500.

Jordan became head coach in 1951 and
the stadium underwent three major expansions in 15 years. More than 40,000 seats,
virtually half of the stadium’s present capacity, were added while Jordan was the coach.

Cliff Hare Stadium became Jordan-Hare
Stadium in 1973. It was the first stadium in
the country to be named for an active coach.

**Overall, Auburn has played 464 games 
in Jordan-Hare Stadium, winning 353, losing 104 and tying seven for a winning 
percentage of .768 against some of the 
best teams in college football.**

**ALEX** GOLESH » HEAD COACH

Alex Golesh, widely regarded as one of college football’s premier offensive minds,
has been named Auburn’s 33rd head coach following three transformative seasons
at South Florida.

The 2025 campaign showcased Golesh’s offensive brilliance as USF posted a 9-3
record and ranked among the nation’s elite in multiple categories. The Bulls finished
No. 2 nationally in total offense (501.7 yards per game) and No. 5 in scoring (43.0
points per game), posting 500-plus yards in eight of nine regular season games.
Under his guidance, quarterback Byrum Brown became just the 12th player in FBS
history to pass for 3,000 yards and rush for 1,000 in a single season.

South Florida’s resurgence included a return to the AP Top 25 for the first time since
2018 and consecutive victories over ranked opponents—defeating Boise State and
Florida to open the season—a program first. The Bulls ranked in the top 25 of 20
national statistical categories spanning offense, defense, and special teams.

Golesh engineered a remarkable turnaround at USF, compiling 23 wins over three
seasons after the Bulls won just four games in the prior three years. His 2023 debut
produced the largest single-season improvement in school history, jumping from 1-11
to 7-6, including a historic 45-0 bowl victory over Syracuse—the largest shutout in
NCAA bowl history. He became the first coach to lead USF to bowl eligibility in his first
three seasons, earning postseason victories in both 2023 and 2024.

Prior to USF, Golesh coordinated Tennessee’s explosive offense from 2021-22,
transforming the Vols from No. 108 nationally in total offense to No. 1 in 2022. His unit
led the country in scoring, total offense, and yards per play while coaching Biletnikoff
Award winner Jalin Hyatt and Maxwell Award finalist Hendon Hooker. The performance earned him Broyles Award finalist recognition.

His coaching journey includes successful stints at Iowa State, UCF, Illinois, and
Toledo, consistently elevating programs through elite recruiting and player development. Born in Moscow, Russia, and raised in Ohio, Golesh earned his bachelor’s
degree from Ohio State in 2006. He and his wife Alexis have two children, Corbin
and Barrett.

Auburn fans who comprised the vast majority of the 55,068 in attendance.

with two minutes remaining. Lagway hit another deep pass down the middle for 27 years to give Baylor first-and-goal at Auburn’s 4-yard line. Three plays
later, on fourth-and-goal from the 5, Lagway found Freeman for a touchdown that pulled the Bears within one point with 11 seconds to play.

of Matthew Klopfenstein inches from the goal line saved the day for Auburn.

takeaway.

remaining in the half.

up again for a 14-yard touchdown that trimmed Auburn’s lead to 10-7 with 1:30 left in the half. Auburn drove 57 yards to the Bears’ 18-yard line, but 
Brown’s second interception of the quarter ended the possession in the closing seconds of the half.

The Tigers’ defense began the second half with a three-and-out and Auburn’s offense followed with a 12-play, 53-yard drive that ended with a missed 
field goal attempt.

found the freshman in the end zone to put the Tigers on top 24-0.

33 yards in 16 seconds, advancing to the Golden Eagles’ 38-yard line.

halftime lead.

Eagles’ end zone for a safety.
