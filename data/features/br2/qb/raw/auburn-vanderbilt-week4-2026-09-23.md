###### STORY LINES

• Auburn trails the all-time series against Vanderbilt, 22-23-1, but owns a 9-1 record against the 
Commodores at home.

• Four of the last six matchups in the series have been decided by seven points or fewer.

• Only one point separates the two teams in the last six matchups - Auburn 125, Vanderbilt 124.

• Auburn is 87-8-4 in Homecoming games all-time. The Tigers have won a program-best 34 consecutive 
Homecoming contests dating back to 1992.

• Auburn’s 39 points vs. Florida last Saturday were the most in a regulation SEC game since a 48-10 win at 
Arkansas on Nov. 11, 2023.

• Byrum Brown accounted for four touchdowns against the Gators - three passing, one rushing - and has 
now accounted for 101 touchdowns in his career, nine more than any other active FBS player.

• The contest marked the 19th of his career in which he has thrown and rushed for at least one score, the 
most among active FBS players.

• Brown, who leads active FBS players with 10,891 career yards of total offense, is the only player in the 
country averaging 250+ passing yards and 60+ rushing yards per game.

• He ranks third in the league with 312.0 yards of total offense per game in 2026.

• Auburn is the only team in the league with multiple players with 17 or more receptions - Keshaun 
Singleton (17) and Chas Nimrod (17).

• Singleton ranks third in the SEC with 12 receptions of 10 or more yards. Thirteen of his 17 receptions 
have resulted in a first down or touchdown.

• Xavier Atkins registered nine tackles, including 2.0 TFL and a sack, vs. Florida. It marked his sixth career 
game with 2.0 or more TFL. His 21.0 TFL the last two seasons are the most in the SEC.

• Elijah Melendez (19), Eric Winters (15) and Atkins (15) rank top three in the SEC in solo tackles. 
• Melendez turned in his second game this season with 10 or more tackles vs. Florida (11), including a

career-high 3.0 TFL and a sack. He leads the SEC in tackles (29) and ranks second in TFL (6.0).

• In three seasons and 27 games under defensive coordinator DJ Durkin, Auburn is allowing 21.2 points per 
game and 111.1 rushing yards per game. The Tigers have allowed the fifth fewest yards per carry (3.0) and 
sixth fewest rushing yards (3,001) in FBS during the span, both good for second in the SEC.

• Auburn’s SEC opponents are a combined 24-3 overall through three weeks of the season.

###### SERIES HISTORY

• Auburn trails the all-time series against Vanderbilt, 
22-23-1, but own a 9-1 record against the 
Commodores at home.

• The lone home loss in the series came in the most 
recent matchup at Jordan-Hare Stadium in 2024.

• Four of the last six matchups in the series have 
been decided by seven points or fewer.

###### HOMECOMING ON THE PLAINS

• Auburn is 87-8-4 in Homecoming games all-time. 
The Tigers have won a program-best 34 consecutive 
Homecoming contests dating back to 1992.

• Saturday marks the first time Auburn has faced 
Vanderbilt on Homecoming.

• Since the inception of the SEC in 1933, Saturday’s 
game marks the 29th time the Tigers have played 
an SEC opponent for Homecoming (22-3-3).

###### UP NEXT

• Auburn faces its first true road test of the season 
with a trip to No. 14/13 Tennessee Saturday, Oct. 3, 
for a X pm kickoff.

• The Tigers lead the all-time series against the 
Volunteers, 29-22-3, with the team’s record in 
Knoxville being 10-14-2.

• The contest will mark Auburn’s first at Neyland 
Stadium since 2013, a 55-23 Auburn win.

• Three of the last five games in the series have 
been decided by six points or fewer.

###### AUBURN IN SEC PLAY

• Auburn holds a 341-287-18 (.542) record in SEC 
play since 1933.

• The Tigers are 150-77-5 (.657) in Jordan-Hare 
Stadium, 128-145-8 (.470) in true road games 
and 63-65-5 (.492) at neutral sites against SEC 
opponents.

###### AUBURN’S ALL-TIME RECORD

• Auburn has an 811-486-47 record in its 134th season 
of football, the 14th most wins among FBS programs.

• The Tigers are 418-113-8 in Auburn, 208-210-20 in 
true road games and 185-163-19 in all other games 
outside of Auburn.

###### JORDAN-HARE STADIUM

• Auburn is 354-104-7 all-time in the 87th season of 
football at Jordan-Hare Stadium (including two wins 
by forfeit against Miss. State).

• The Tigers have had a winning home record 70 
times in the history of JHS, including 21 of the last 
26 seasons.

###### HOME FIELD ADVANTAGE

• Saturday’s game vs. Vanderbilt marks the 25th 
consecutive sellout at Jordan-Hare Stadium dating 
back to the beginning of the 2023 season.

• The 18th consecutive sellout vs. Georgia 
surpassed the previous program record (17) set 
from 2013-16.

###### DEBUT DUBS

• Head coach Alex Golesh became the 15th head 
coach in program history to win the first two games 
of his Auburn career.

###### CLOSE CALL

• Auburn’s 17-16 win in the season opener vs. 
Baylor marked the program’s first one-point win 
since defeating No. 11 Alabama 28-27 in the 2010 
Iron Bowl.

• The Tigers trailed the Crimson Tide 24-0 midway 
through the second quarter in Tuscaloosa but 
outscored its rival 28-3 the rest of the game to 
secure the win.

• The win was Auburn’s first win in a onepossession game since the 43-41 (4OT) win vs. 
Texas A&M on Nov. 23, 2024.

• Going back to 2021, the Tigers were 5-17 in one 
possession games before Saturday’s win.

• Team’s coached by Alex Golesh are 19-1 when 
leading at halftime and 24-1 when leading after the 
third quarter.

###### UBER-EXPERIENCED STAFF IN PLACE

• Auburn’s first-year coaching staff includes 93 
years of Power 4 coordinator and coach experience 
including 10 of the 12 having coached previous 
seasons at the P4 level.

###### ALL-AMERICAN LINEBACKER XAVIER ATKINS

• Junior Xavier Atkins is first returning All-American 
on defense at Auburn since Tracy Rocker, who 
was a two-time All-American in 1987-88. Atkins has 
received multiple Preseason All-America honors for 
2026 following his stellar 2025 campaign.

• Atkins and Byrum Brown gave Auburn two players 
on the Maxwell Award Preseason List, one of five 
programs in the SEC with multiple selections.
• Atkins (No. 16) and Brown (No. 80) were both on 
On3’s ‘Way-too-Early” Top 100 list for the 2026 season. 
Atkins was the highest rated linebacker on the list.
• Atkins finished the 2025 season with 9.0 sacks and 
17.0 TFL - the most tackles for loss by an Auburn 
player since 2010.

###### PICKING UP WHERE HE LEFT OFF

• Coming off his 9.0 sack, 17.0 TFL effort in 2025, 
Atkins registered a sack on the second defensive 
play in the season opener against Baylor, the 10th 
sack of his Auburn career, and has 4.0 TFL and 2.0 
sacks through three games this season.

• He registered a season-high nine tackles and 2.0 
TFL vs. Florida, marking the sixth time since last 
season he has registered 2.0+ TFL.

• Atkins is sixth among active FBS players with 0.95 
TFL per game in his career, and his 21.0 TFL since 
the beginning of 2025 are the most in the SEC.

###### SUPER SOPHOMORES

• Led by linebacker Elijah Melendez and safety Eric 
Winters, who lead SEC underclassmen in tackles, 
the sophomore class has accounted for 93 tackles, 
13.0 TFL, 4.0 sacks, a forced fumble and an 
interception in the first three games of the season.
• Winters’ forced fumble vs. Baylor was the first of 
his career and Jared Smith’s interception against the 
Bears was the first of his career.

• The turnovers came on back-to-back drives in the 
first quarter, marking the first time with two firstquarter turnovers since doing so vs. Samford in 2019.

###### WINTERS WENT WILD

• Winters tallied a career-high 11 tackles and 
2.0 TFL to go along with his first career forced 
fumble and the game saving tackle on a two-point 
conversion with :09 left in the opener vs. Baylor. 
• Winters became the first Auburn safety with 10+ 
tackles and 2.0+ TFL in a game since Jeremiah 
Dinson did so vs. Oregon to open the 2019 season. 
• He was tied for first nationally with nine solo 
tackles in week one and went on to be named the 
SEC Co-Defensive Player of the Week and both the 
Bronko Nagurski and Lott IMPACT Trophy National 
Defensive Player of the Week.

###### MELENDEZ’S MONSTER GAME

• Melendez registered a game-high and career-high 
12 tackles with 1.5 TFL in the season-opening win 
vs. Baylor and is tied for the league with 29 tackles 
- the most by an Auburn player through three weeks 
since Zakoby McClain (32) in 2020.

• He has double digit tackles in two of the first three 
games, coming off an 11 tackle, 3.0 TFL effort vs. 
Florida, and is second in the league with 6.0 TFL.
• His 12 tackles vs. Baylor were the most by an 
Auburn player in a season opener since Cam Riley 
recorded 15 tackles vs. Mercer in 2022.

• He teamed up with Winters to become the first duo 
since 2020 to record 10+ tackles in the opener.

• Melendez came up with a two-yard TFL on third 
and two to force a fourth down and eventually 
turnover on downs at the start of the fourth quarter.
• The next drive, he broke up a pass in the endzone 
that would’ve resulted in Baylor’s go-ahead score.

###### KEEP THE CHAINS MOVING

• Auburn’s offense has converted 23 of 45 third 
downs this season (51 percent), good for the fourth 
best clip in the SEC and 23rd best clip in FBS.

- vs. Baylor: 10-for-18

- vs. USM: 7-for-15

- vs. UF: 6-for-13

• Against Baylor, QB Byrum Brown went 8-for-12 
with 110 yards and convereted seven first downs. 
On the game-winning drive, he went 4-for-4 with 49 
yards and added 18 yards rushing (GW TD).

###### ALL EYES ON BYRUM BROWN

• Auburn QB Byrum Brown was only QB in the 
country in 2025 to grade above 85 as both a passer 
and runner according to PFF.

• He entered the year having thrown and rushed for 
a TD in eight straight games, and he leads all active 
QBs with 19 games with both a rushing and passing 
touchdown in his career after throwing for three and 
rushing for another vs. Florida.

• Brown, who has thrown for 8,446 yards and 
rushed for 2,445 yards in his career, is the only 
active FBS QB who has thrown for 8,000+ yards, 
and rushed for 2,000+ yards.

• He is fifth among active QBs with 66 passing 
touchdowns and second with 33 rushing 
touchdowns. He is the only active player and just 
the 29th player since 1956 to have 60+ passing TD 
and 30+ rushing TD.

• Brown became the 22nd Auburn quarterback since 
1969 to make his team debut in a season opener. 
Auburn’s record in those games is 18-4.

• Of Auburn’s 46 starting quarterbacks since 1969,

conference opponents.

**JORDAN-HARE STADIUM // 88,043**
**24 CONSECUTIVE SELLOUTS**

Pat Dye Field at Jordan-Hare Stadium, the 
nation’s 11th-largest on-campus stadium with 
a capacity of 88,043, is in its 83rd year as 
home to the Auburn Tigers.

On football Saturdays in Auburn, Jordan-
Hare Stadium becomes Alabama’s sixth-largest city. A record number of 63,500 season 
tickets were sold before the 2023 and 2024 
seasons.

The stadium is named for Ralph “Shug” 
Jordan, Auburn’s all-time winningest football 
coach, and Clifford Leroy Hare, a member of 
Auburn’s first football team, president of the 
old Southern Conference and longtime chairman of Auburn’s Faculty Athletic Committee.

Legendary coach Pat Dye has also had 
his name forever attached to Jordan-Hare 
Stadium, as the turf officially became known 
as Pat Dye Field at Jordan-Hare Stadium 
prior to the 2005 Iron Bowl. Dye, who served 
as coach and athletics director at Auburn 
(1981-92) and was inducted into the College 
Football Hall of Fame (2005), led Auburn to a 
99-39-4 record and four SEC titles in his 12 
seasons on the Plains.

**Auburn’s current string of 24 consecutive sellout crowds at Jordan-Hare** 
**Stadium, including the Mercer game, 
breaks the previous program record 
streak of 17 consecutive sellout crowds, 
set between the 2013 Georgia game and 
the 2016 Clemson game.**

What is now Jordan-Hare Stadium was 
dedicated November 30, 1939, at the Auburn-
Florida game. Babe McGehee, who passed 
away in 2005, scored the first touchdown 
in what was then called “Auburn Stadium,” 
by catching a pass from Dick McGowen. 
McGowen, who later coached at Auburn 
under Jordan, kicked the extra point and 
Auburn tied Florida, 7-7.

That first stadium held 7,500 seats and 
consisted of what is now the bottom part of 
the lower west stands. When the stadium was 
renamed “Cliff Hare Stadium” in 1949, 14,000 
seats—the present lower east stands—had 
been added, raising capacity to 21,500.

Jordan became head coach in 1951 and 
the stadium underwent three major expansions in 15 years. More than 40,000 seats, 
virtually half of the stadium’s present capacity, were added while Jordan was the coach.

Cliff Hare Stadium became Jordan-Hare 
Stadium in 1973. It was the first stadium in 
the country to be named for an active coach.

**Overall, Auburn has played 465 games 
in Jordan-Hare Stadium, winning 353, losing 105 and tying seven for a winning 
percentage of .768 against some of the 
best teams in college football.**

**ALEX** GOLESH » HEAD COACH

Alex Golesh, widely regarded as one of college football’s premier offensive minds, 
has been named Auburn’s 33rd head coach following three transformative seasons 
at South Florida.

The 2025 campaign showcased Golesh’s offensive brilliance as USF posted a 9-3 
record and ranked among the nation’s elite in multiple categories. The Bulls finished 
No. 2 nationally in total offense (501.7 yards per game) and No. 5 in scoring (43.0 
points per game), posting 500-plus yards in eight of nine regular season games. 
Under his guidance, quarterback Byrum Brown became just the 12th player in FBS 
history to pass for 3,000 yards and rush for 1,000 in a single season.

South Florida’s resurgence included a return to the AP Top 25 for the first time since 
2018 and consecutive victories over ranked opponents—defeating Boise State and 
Florida to open the season—a program first. The Bulls ranked in the top 25 of 20 
national statistical categories spanning offense, defense, and special teams.

Golesh engineered a remarkable turnaround at USF, compiling 23 wins over three 
seasons after the Bulls won just four games in the prior three years. His 2023 debut 
produced the largest single-season improvement in school history, jumping from 1-11 
to 7-6, including a historic 45-0 bowl victory over Syracuse—the largest shutout in 
NCAA bowl history. He became the first coach to lead USF to bowl eligibility in his first 
three seasons, earning postseason victories in both 2023 and 2024.

Prior to USF, Golesh coordinated Tennessee’s explosive offense from 2021-22, 
transforming the Vols from No. 108 nationally in total offense to No. 1 in 2022. His unit 
led the country in scoring, total offense, and yards per play while coaching Biletnikoff 
Award winner Jalin Hyatt and Maxwell Award finalist Hendon Hooker. The performance earned him Broyles Award finalist recognition.

His coaching journey includes successful stints at Iowa State, UCF, Illinois, and 
Toledo, consistently elevating programs through elite recruiting and player development. Born in Moscow, Russia, and raised in Ohio, Golesh earned his bachelor’s 
degree from Ohio State in 2006. He and his wife Alexis have two children, Corbin 
and Barrett.
